# Family Transition Tool — Problem & Audience (the anchor doc)

*Locked 2026-05-23. The single source of truth for what we're solving and for whom. Keep it tight.*

---

## One line
**A tool that helps a family rebuild their life and their belonging when they move somewhere new — and turns each arriving family from a stranger into a contributor to the community.**

## The problem (locked)
> When a family moves somewhere new, they lose their network overnight and have to rebuild everything from scratch — the **practical** (daycare, GP, transport, schools) *and* the **unspoken** (how things work here, the local culture, where they belong). **Migrant families feel it hardest**, and the member who didn't move for a job or school has **no natural way in at all**.

## The A1 insight (the thing we noticed)
People who move *for* something — a job, study, sport, a religious community — arrive with a built-in entry point. **The people with no entry point are invisible**: the accompanying partner, the family that just landed, anyone "unestablished." Everyone builds tools to help newcomers *receive*; almost nothing helps them *belong by contributing*. And the knowledge that would help them **already exists in the community** — it just has no way to reach them or be passed on.

## Who it's for

**Primary audience:** relocating **families**, with **migrant families** as the sharp focus (acute need, EDS Pillar 2 fit). Extensible to any domestic mover ("Martin changing suburbs") — that's the uptake/reusability upside, not the pitch lead.

**Hero persona — Priya, 34.** Accountant in Mumbai. Moved to Melbourne 4 months ago when her husband took a 482 tech role. *He* has 40 new colleagues; *their daughter* has a class of 25. **Priya knows no one**, can't easily work on her visa, and her whole social world is now one person. Back home she ran a community kitchen. Here, she has nowhere to put any of that.

**Segmentation — who's in, who's out:**
| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Core target** | The "unestablished" — relocating families with no roots; *especially the member with no work/school pathway* (the trailing partner) |
| 🔸 Adjacent (not the lead) | Kids (school = pathway), domestic suburb-movers (broaden later), refugees (higher acuity, different product) |
| ❌ Not our user | The person who moved **for** the job — they already have the entry point |

## What we're building (two layers, ONE engine)
1. **Transition plan — the utility / hook.** The practical "set up your new life" (daycare, schools, parks, GP, groceries, transport, library) **+ lived cultural-navigation knowledge** (how self-checkout works, what's rude to say, what the library actually offers). Adapts for newcomers (language/culture).
2. **Living community memory — the resilience / ground-up layer.** Residents, businesses and prior newcomers contribute stories & knowledge; **every new family adds to the story instead of just consuming it.**

## How it actually solves it (the give-to-get loop)
> **You share your story → you get your transition plan → your story powers the next family's.**

Contribution is the **front door, not an add-on** — that's what fuses the two layers into one product (answers the "does the community bit feel bolted on?" risk). The same community-contributed knowledge is *both* the utility (real, local, lived answers) *and* the resilience (the community building its own onboarding fabric).

## Why this isn't "just ChatGPT/Gemini"
Generic LLMs already do empathy, generic advice, translation, drafting. Our defensible layer is **Grounding + Action + Continuity + Community Memory**:
- **Grounding:** real, current, hyperlocal *lived* knowledge LLMs hallucinate or lack.
- **Action:** it does the steps (books, messages, reminds), not just advises.
- **Continuity:** a companion across the months-long settling-in journey.
- **Community memory:** a resident-contributed knowledge base that compounds — something a stateless chatbot can never accrue.
> **Don't let the demo become "look, it found a daycare"** — that's the commoditised, ChatGPT-able trap. Lead with lived cultural knowledge + the contribution loop.

## Resilience line (answers the prompt verbatim)
> It's *resilience built from the ground up by residents and communities* because the knowledge and connections come **from** residents and prior newcomers — not handed down top-down. Each arriving family becomes a contributor who strengthens the fabric for the next. The trailing partner — the **least resilient** member of the household (whole world = one person) — becomes connected and contributing. **That compounding connective tissue is the resilience.**
